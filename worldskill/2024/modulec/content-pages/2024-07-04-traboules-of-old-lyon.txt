Traboules are covered passageways that run through courtyards and staircases to connect one street to the next.

They were opened in the fourth century so that the inhabitants of Lugdunum could fetch water from the Saone without walking around every block.

traboules-passage.jpeg

Silk workers later used them to carry finished fabric from the workshop to the merchant without exposing the delicate cloth to the rain.

There are more than four hundred traboules in Lyon and roughly forty of them are open to visitors, marked by a small bronze plaque near the door.
